About IMP — Introduction

The Integrity Management Program is a preventive anti-corruption measure designed to help public sector institutions build, improve, reinforce, and sustain a culture of integrity. It focuses on acceptable values, principles, and standards of good governance.

The program supports institutions in managing the renewal and improvement of individual and institutional values, principles of conduct, and standards of performance. Its intended outcome is meaningful change in the bureaucracy and stronger public trust.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Integrity Management Program?

The IMP is a preventive anti-corruption measure of the government. It provides a systematic and outcome-based approach for building and sustaining a culture of integrity in public sector institutions.

Why is integrity important?

Integrity supports honest, responsible, and trustworthy public service. It helps institutions uphold good governance, address vulnerabilities, and maintain public trust.

Who can use or benefit from IMP?

The program is intended for public sector institutions and their officials and personnel. The official program describes implementation across government institutions, with approaches suited to each organization.

What are integrity measures?

Integrity measures are policies, programs, and systems that create an enabling environment for integrity management in an organization. They respond to identified issues, vulnerabilities, and concerns, and help put integrity values, principles, and standards into practice.

What is a corruption risk?

A corruption risk is an integrity risk or corruption issue that can be identified from observable gaps, issues, concerns, or flaws in a system. If it is not adequately managed and addressed, it may develop into actual corruption. Examples include bribery, information trading, collusion, conflicts of interest, patronage, and favoritism.

What are the steps in developing integrity measures?

The four steps are: analyze the risks; identify the decision criteria; come up with more than one measure; and adopt the best measures.
